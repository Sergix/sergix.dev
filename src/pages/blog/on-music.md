- q: does music have an inherent theology? not lyrics, but style/genre/sound
- thesis: there is not one style/genre/sound of music that is more glorifying to God than others, except for that which is individually limited by one's conscience.

- t0: definitions
- music, worship, conscience, holiness

- t1: association with worldliness or evil
- q: what characteristics are often associated with "wordliness"? ("fundamentalist" cultural assoc.)
    -> pop, rock, hip-hop, rap, jazz, electronic, country(?)
          - accusations: hedonism, superficiality, lust, violence
       NOT classical, folk, movie soundtracks, classical renditions of pop/rock
          - "typically": happiness, joy, comfort, ease-of-listening, peace/calm

    sample genre: metal
        - common association and use: darkness, despair, demons, aggression, violence, grotesque, existential, brooding
        - my associations (Christian bands like WATG): tension, awe, excitement, finality, 
        - common components (though not necessary):
           - loud
           - fast, driving rhythm
           - complex
           - intense
           - dissonance/distortion
           - harsh/dramatic vocals
           - harmonic minor
        -> are any of these components sinful or less glorifying to God?
        -> if no, then why would the whole not be? could these same elements be used to explicitly glorify God?

        -> what is the danger?
            - you let the music crush you
            - you desire certain sinful emotions as a satisfaction apart from God, rather than bringing you near Him

    even with this example, though, metal can't be adequately confined; i.e. "drone" metal tends to use heavy riffs with spacious reverb that is much slower
    -> so then should it be excluded on a song rather than genre basis? may be a better application for things like classical

    -> q: is association universal or individual?
        -> both? certain genres are chosen for certain reasons, to express ideas. we obv intuit this; see movie soundtracks
        -> in a movie soundtrack: parts of the story may contain confusion/anger, and the music expresses those emotions well
        \\-> listening to music itself for pleasure, is there a difference in listening disassociated from a narrative?

    - q: what about extreme examples like harsh noise, black metal, ...?
       - atypical examples: experimental classical with surrealist/harsh/unusual elements (George Crumb)

       -> should you know the artist's intent? if the intent is demonic and anti-God, should you stay away from it?
          - maybe the individual songs itself, yes, even if lyricless; but the genre, not necessarily; see meat offered to idols
          -> clarity in meaning b/w words and music; subjectivity in difference

- q: why do we associate them with worldliness?
- q: is association irredeemable, or at best unwise?
- q: what if i decided to create a "worldly" kind of music, like metal, as a Christian? would I be in sin?

(prob hardest q)
- t2: the inherent properties of music
- q: is it right to say that some music "sounds" evil/unholy?
- q: what properties of music do we tend to associate with those types of sounds? i.e. why are genres chosen?
- q: what emotions do they evoke? is it sinful that they evoke those types of emotions?
    - q: if i am walking through the store and a song is playing that makes me feel angry, have i sinned? or is it the intentional seeking of that emotion?
    -> the heart is what matters; are you satisfying a longing in your heart with something to replace God's satisfaction and comfort?
- q: if we make the assumption that music genres are not universally sinful, what about for the individual?
    -> let's say a style of music bring back memories that evoke lustful thoughts. then that would be wrong for the individual, because it tempts him to sin by that association

- t3: music is not a special, reserved category under God's creation compared to others of life's enjoyments
- q: what does the Bible say about music and its uses?
- q: how are we to enjoy any type of music? what is unique about music in its expression and universality?
- q: does this differ in any special way from others of life's enjoyments? i.e. why do we not say some sports are more "holy" than others?

- conclusion
- q: what does it mean to be free in your conscience? how is that empowering to the Christian to live for God's glory?
- q: why not just "stick with Christian music and you'll be fine"?
